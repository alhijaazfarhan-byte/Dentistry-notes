1. 🦷 Basic Tooth Anatomy

A tooth is a specialized mineralized organ located within the oral cavity. Its major structures are enamel, dentin, pulp and cementum. Understanding these layers is essential for understanding caries, restorative dentistry and endodontics.

🦷
ENAMEL — outer protective layer
DENTIN — bulk of the tooth
PULP — nerves + blood vessels

Enamel

Enamel is the highly mineralized outer covering of the anatomical crown. It is the hardest tissue in the human body. Unlike many other tissues, mature enamel has no living cells and therefore has very limited biological repair capacity.

Dentin

Dentin lies beneath enamel and cementum. It forms most of the tooth structure. Dentinal tubules extend through dentin and help explain dentinal sensitivity.

Pulp

The dental pulp is a soft connective tissue containing blood vessels, nerves and cells. Pulpal inflammation is clinically important because it can produce characteristic tooth pain.

Cementum

Cementum covers the anatomical root and provides attachment for periodontal ligament fibers.

🩺 Clinical connection:
If dental caries progresses through enamel and reaches dentin, bacterial products can travel through dentinal tubules. The patient may begin experiencing sensitivity, particularly to cold or sweet stimuli.

2. 🧠 Understanding Dental Caries

Dental caries is a biofilm-mediated, sugar-driven, multifactorial disease that results in the net loss of mineral from dental hard tissues.

Important risk factors

  • Frequent intake of fermentable carbohydrates
  • Dental plaque/biofilm
  • Reduced salivary protection
  • Inadequate fluoride exposure
  • Poor oral hygiene
  • Deep pits and fissures
🩺 Clinical scenario:
A student frequently drinks sugary beverages throughout the day. Even though the total amount of sugar consumed is not enormous, the repeated exposure gives plaque bacteria multiple opportunities to produce acid. Caries risk therefore increases.
